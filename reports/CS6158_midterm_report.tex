\section{Research Goal and Progress}

Our project asks how to allocate a fixed budget among runtime monitor-creation
locations using historical execution frequency, cost, useful traces, violation
reports, and redundancy. The primary research question is whether budget-aware
selection preserves more violations than simple heuristics or \emph{Valg}, an
adaptive online monitor-creation policy, at comparable \emph{realized} overhead.
We originally proposed evaluation on held-out executions, including unique-trace
and violation recall, runtime and memory, with software evolution and exploration
as extensions.

At midterm, we have \textbf{implemented five selection baselines and a
reproducible evaluation pipeline}, and performed \textbf{offline selection
experiments on historical TraceMOP monitor-profile data for two real Java
projects}. In parallel, we reproduced a subset of the official Valg artifact on
four Java projects. The two studies serve different purposes: the offline
experiment evaluates budget-allocation rules under a historical proxy cost,
whereas the artifact reproduction validates the executable monitoring
infrastructure and establishes preliminary realized-time baselines. Our budgeted
policies have not yet been deployed inside Valg or compared with it at matched
measured cost.

\section{Implemented Modules and Methods}

The Python selection pipeline reads per-group historical information and
produces selected group IDs, budget usage, recall metrics, CSV tables, and plots.
Five strategies are implemented: \textbf{random} feasible selection (30 draws per
budget in the real-trace evaluation); \textbf{frequency ranking};
\textbf{standalone utility/cost ranking}; \textbf{redundancy-aware greedy}, which
maximizes incremental distinct-trace gain per unit cost; and \textbf{additive
knapsack}, which maximizes the sum of standalone trace counts using a discretized
dynamic program. The knapsack surrogate can double-count traces shared by
several groups; it is not an exact optimum for distinct-union coverage.

For historical trace IDs $T_i$ attached to a candidate group $i$, a selected set
$S$, and a proxy cost $c_i$, the current offline experiment approximates
\begin{equation}
  \max_{S}\quad \left|\bigcup_{i\in S}T_i\right|
  \quad \text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{i\in S}c_i\le B,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{TraceRecall}(S)=
  \frac{|\cup_{i\in S}T_i|}{|\cup_iT_i|}.
\end{equation}
Here $c_i$ is the workbook's \texttt{cost\_proxy\_event\_checks}, \emph{not
execution time}. Candidate rows represent inferred location-by-specification
groups; they are not yet verified as independently switchable monitor-creation
sites. Real-trace violation weighting is not yet supported because the workbook
lacks reliable location-attributed violation sets.

For the artifact study, we used the official Valg scripts in a prepared Docker
environment and pinned project commits. Each Java-mode run executes the same
project tests under no runtime verification (no-RV), ValgJ selective monitoring,
and JavaMOP full monitoring. The trace-mode pair compares ValgT with TraceMOP.
Raw logs, elapsed times, extracted violation counts, trace summaries, and the
scripts used to aggregate the measurements are retained in the repository.

\section{Experiments and Preliminary Results}

\subsection{Historical Real-Trace Offline Evaluation}

We processed the partner-provided aggregation for \texttt{javadbf} (153
location-by-specification groups, 278 observed distinct trace IDs) and
\texttt{jlemmagen} (66 groups, 2,115 trace IDs). Budgets were 10, 20, 30, 50, and
80\% of summed event-check proxies. Random selection used 30 draws per budget.
Table~\ref{tab:offline} reports the 20\% result; the denominator is the union of
trace IDs already present in the historical records.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Offline unique-trace recall at a 20\% event-check proxy budget.}
  \label{tab:offline}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    Strategy & \texttt{javadbf} & \texttt{jlemmagen} \\
    \midrule
    Random (30-draw mean) & 86.51\% & 65.56\% \\
    Frequency & 44.96\% & 88.84\% \\
    Utility/cost & 97.84\% & 99.67\% \\
    Redundancy-aware greedy & 97.84\% & 99.67\% \\
    Additive knapsack & 89.57\% & 99.57\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The cost-aware approaches preserved most previously observed distinct traces at
this proxy budget. Greedy and static utility/cost tied on both workloads, so the
current results do not establish a benefit from explicitly modeling redundancy.
Knapsack can perform worse because its additive objective rewards overlapping
traces repeatedly. Frequency selections can be non-monotone because each budget
independently scans a fixed ordering and admits any group that fits.

that ValgJ was slower. Because each cell is a single run, the table establishes
artifact execution rather than a statistically stable performance estimate.

ValgJ was faster on three of four projects, with a median reduction of 31.2\%.
Within every ValgJ/JavaMOP pair, the extracted logs contained the same number of
distinct violation rows, although ValgJ produced fewer violation occurrences.
We therefore report preservation of the observed violation signatures, not a
general violation-recall claim.

instances while retaining most unique traces observed by TraceMOP.

The left panel compares end-to-end test time under no-RV, ValgJ selective
monitoring, and JavaMOP full monitoring. ValgJ is faster than JavaMOP for
\texttt{xeger}, \texttt{route53-infima}, and especially \texttt{jlemmagen}, but

repeated traces, together with a much smaller loss of unique behavior, is
consistent with Valg's goal of avoiding redundant monitoring work. Nevertheless,
the trace result covers only one project, and both panels report single runs, so
the figure validates our setup rather than reproducing the paper's full
evaluation or proving statistical performance gains.

\section{Challenges, Risks, and What We Learned}

First, \textbf{violation attribution is missing}: historical reports identify
violations but do not show whether each proposed location would independently
detect them. Second, \textbf{proxy cost is not realized overhead}: global
instrumentation, shared monitoring work, and interactions between selected
groups can invalidate an additive runtime interpretation. Third, inferred
location-by-specification groups may not correspond to independently controllable
creation-site switches. Fourth, the current real-trace data have no held-out
evaluation split. Fifth, the artifact timings are single runs and only one
project has a complete ValgT/TraceMOP pair. We therefore cannot yet claim
generalization, memory savings, or superiority over Valg.
